Book: Deep Work by Cal Newport

📚 5 Things I Learned

  • Deep work vs. shallow work: deep work is focused, undistracted work that pushes your cognitive limit; shallow work (emails, idle meetings) is easy and creates little value.
  • Focus is a formula: high-quality work = time spent × intensity of focus. Switching tasks leaves "mental residue" that lowers focus.
  • Distraction is everywhere: open offices, instant messaging and social media (with unpredictable "variable rewards") make deep work difficult.
  • Make it a ritual: pick a schedule (seclusion, periodic, daily or ad hoc). Beginners manage about 1 hour a day; even experts rarely exceed 4 hours.
  • Train and recover: let yourself be bored, track leading metrics, and end each day with a shutdown ritual so your brain can rest.

💡 3 Lessons I Want to Apply

  1. Protect a daily block of focus time for my most important study and coding work.
  2. Remove distractions – especially my phone and social media – instead of relying on willpower.
  3. Rest on purpose: shut work down at the end of the day so I recover and focus better tomorrow.

🛠️ 3 Action Plans

Plan 1 · Lesson 1

Use the daily schedule: a fixed 2-hour deep work block every morning, in one dedicated study spot, with a goal written down before I start.

Plan 2 · Lesson 2

Keep my phone in another room during deep work, use a website blocker, and run a 30-day social media experiment to see if I miss it.

Plan 3 · Lesson 3

Do a shutdown ritual each evening (review to-dos, plan tomorrow, say "All done, Alhamdulillah") and a short weekly review of my deep work hours.
